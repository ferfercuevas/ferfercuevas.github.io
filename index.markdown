<p>
  I am currently a Master's student in Cognitive Neuroscience at Radboud University, working on the structural correlates of early language development in the <a href="https://www.stephanieforkel.com/team" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Clinical Neuroanatomy of Language Group</a> at the Donders Institute, and as a research assistant on the <em>Wired for Growth</em> project at the <a href="https://www.mpi.nl/department/language-development/3/people" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Language Development Department</a> of the Max Planck Institute for Psycholinguistics.
</p>

<p>
  What fascinates me is how brain structure and the exposome together shape the early years of cognition. These relationships are rarely simple: development cannot be read off the volume of a single region or pinned to one feature of a child's environment, because the factors interact in complex ways that even vary from one individual to the next. This is precisely why I am drawn to computational models, and to two intertwined threads in particular: using machine learning to study the relationships between cognitive functions, and using artificial neural networks to model how cognition unfolds over time. Learning to work across both is where much of my energy goes right now. Understanding these mechanisms in detail, I believe, is what lets us promote adaptive, personalized development in younger generations.
</p>

<p>
  My long-term goal is to bring this understanding into society and change how we think about cognitive development. Once we can map how minds grow and how they shape one another, the benefits don't stop at the individual: they reach the families and communities those individuals are woven into. 
</p>
